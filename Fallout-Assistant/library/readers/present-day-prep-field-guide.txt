PRESENT-DAY PREP // BUY + ROTATE + STORE

PURPOSE
Build ordinary emergency capacity before an incident. This is a planning and rotation guide,
not a shopping endorsement. Official alerts, product labels, recalls, expiration dates, local
law, medical requirements, and the needs of the people and animals in your household control.

BUILD IN LAYERS
1. Carry layer: identification, essential medicine, contact card, light, water, charging lead.
2. Vehicle/work layer: weather-appropriate clothing, water, shelf-stable food, first aid,
   visibility gear, local paper map, power bank, and a way to receive official alerts.
3. Home layer: water, food requiring little fuel or water, sanitation, lighting, radio,
   first aid, prescription planning, fire safety, documents, cash, tools, and pet supplies.
4. Evacuation layer: portable copies of the most critical home supplies and records.

WHERE TO BUY
Prefer established local grocers, pharmacies, hardware stores, outdoor suppliers, and the
manufacturer or an authorized dealer for safety-critical equipment. Compare the item against
an official checklist before buying. Avoid mystery batteries, counterfeit medical products,
unrated fuel containers, and equipment without an accessible manual or replacement parts.

ROTATION AND STORAGE
Mark acquisition and replace-by dates. Use the oldest suitable stock first. Inspect quarterly
and before hazard seasons. Store food, water, medicine, fuel, and batteries according to their
labels; separate incompatible materials. Keep supplies dry, cool where required, pest-resistant,
and off flood-prone floors. Never store fuels or combustion equipment in occupied living space.

DISTRIBUTED KITS
Do not put every essential in one container or one room. Keep deliberately smaller lawful kits
at home, work, and in vehicles, and record their location in Inventory. A distributed kit is for
availability and resilience, not concealment. Tell responsible household members where critical
supplies, utility shutoffs, fire extinguishers, and medical information are located.

FIREARMS AND AMMUNITION
This shelf addresses lawful safe storage only. Use a secure storage device accessible only to
authorized responsible adults. Store unloaded firearms locked; store ammunition locked and
separately; keep keys and combinations controlled. Maintain identifying records apart from the
firearm. Follow current federal, state, and local law and the ATF source in this shelf.

REVIEW LOOP
Count -> inspect -> test -> rotate -> document -> train. Record shortages and dates in F10
Inventory, then use F8 Captain's Log for drills, sleep, health condition, and daily travel.
