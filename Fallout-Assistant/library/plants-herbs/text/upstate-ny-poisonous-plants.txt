• We suggest you learn the names of all plants in
  your home and yard.
• Don’t be surprised to find that some parts of
  the same plant, like asparagus, rhubarb, or
  potato, can be poisonous while other parts are
  eatable.
• If you think someone might be poisoned, call
  the Upstate New York Poison Center.


Plant poisoning can make you sick.
It can also cause redness or itching of
the skin.

What if a poisoning occurs?
• Remove any portion left in the mouth.
• Call our poison center.
• Give a brief history of what happened.
• Our Poison Specialist will tell you what to do next.


POISON PREVENTION TIPS
1. Teach your children never to put leaves, stems,
                                                            FA S T. F R E E . C O N F I D E N T I A L .
   bark, seeds, nuts or berries from any plant in their
   mouths, as many can be poisonous.
2. Store poisonous house plants, bulbs and seeds out
   of sight and out of reach.
3. Learn the names of the plants in your home and yard.
4. Don’t assume that a plant is non-toxic to you if a
   bird or other wildlife is eating it.
5. “Cooking” poisonous plants doesn’t destroy
   poisons in the plant.
